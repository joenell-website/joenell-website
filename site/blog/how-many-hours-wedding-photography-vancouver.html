Introduction

Your wedding day moves quickly.

One moment you're getting ready with your closest friends and family. Then you're walking down the aisle, hugging people you haven't seen in years, taking portraits, celebrating with your guests, and suddenly the day you've spent months planning is almost over.

That's one of the reasons choosing the right amount of wedding photography coverage matters.

When couples start looking at wedding photographers in Vancouver, one of the first questions that often comes up is:

"How many hours of photography do we actually need?"

The answer isn't the same for every wedding.

A small ceremony followed by an intimate dinner may only need a few hours of photography. A larger wedding with getting-ready coverage, a ceremony, family portraits, couple portraits, cocktail hour, reception and dancing may benefit from much more extensive coverage.

The goal isn't to book the maximum number of hours.

The goal is to have enough time to tell the story of your wedding day without feeling rushed or missing the moments that matter most.

At Joenell Photography, I take a natural, story-driven approach to wedding photography. My focus is not simply on documenting the major events, but on preserving the emotions, relationships, details and little moments that make your wedding uniquely yours.

This guide will help you understand what different amounts of wedding photography coverage can include, how to think about your timeline, and how to decide what makes sense for your Vancouver wedding.

Part 1: Why Wedding Photography Coverage Matters

Why Wedding Photography Coverage Matters?

Choosing your wedding photography coverage isn't simply about deciding between a few hours or a full day. It's really about deciding which parts of your wedding story you want preserved.

Every wedding has a beginning, middle and end.

The beginning might be the quiet excitement of getting ready, the final touches to your dress, your parents seeing you for the first time, or your wedding party helping you prepare.

The middle could include your ceremony, family photographs, portraits with your partner, cocktail hour and all the little interactions happening around you.

Then comes the celebration — speeches, dinner, first dances, laughter, dancing and the moments you may not even realize happened because you're busy enjoying the day.

Your photography coverage should reflect the story you want to remember.

Think about the moments that matter most

Before deciding how many hours to book, think about the parts of your wedding day that you would genuinely miss seeing again.

Do you want photographs of the morning preparations?

Would you like your photographer to document the moment your parents or wedding party see you dressed for the first time?

Do you want photographs of your ceremony and the emotions immediately afterward?

Would you like plenty of time for relaxed portraits together?

Do you want your photographer to stay through speeches, your first dance and the beginning of your reception?

These questions are often more useful than simply asking, "How many hours does a wedding photographer need?"

Wedding photography should not feel rushed

One of the biggest advantages of having enough coverage is simply having room to breathe.

When a photographer has very limited time, the schedule can become compressed. Portraits may need to happen quickly, family photographs may feel rushed, and there may be little opportunity to capture the spontaneous moments happening between the major events.

With more time, the photography can feel more natural.

There is room to step away for a few quiet portraits. There is time to photograph details. There is time to capture your guests interacting with each other rather than only documenting the formal parts of the day.

That's particularly important for a documentary and storytelling approach, where some of the most meaningful photographs aren't necessarily planned.

Part 2: What Can Be Covered in 3 Hours?

What Can Be Covered in 3 Hours of Wedding Photography?

Three hours of photography can work beautifully for certain intimate weddings, elopements and smaller celebrations.

The key is having a clear idea of what you want photographed.

With three hours, you generally won't be able to document an entire wedding day from getting ready through the end of the reception. Instead, the coverage should be focused on the most meaningful portion of the celebration.

For example, three hours could allow time for:

  • The final moments before the ceremony
  • The ceremony itself
  • Family and group photographs
  • Couple portraits
  • A small portion of the celebration afterward

This can be a great option for couples having a short ceremony followed by an intimate gathering.

It can also work particularly well when photography is focused on the ceremony and portraits, rather than the entire wedding-day story.

When 3 hours may be enough

Consider shorter coverage if you're planning:

  • An intimate wedding
  • A small ceremony
  • A simple elopement
  • A courthouse or civil ceremony
  • A short celebration
  • A wedding where you don't need getting-ready coverage
  • A reception that doesn't need to be photographed

The important thing is not how many hours other couples book.

It's whether those hours cover the moments that matter most to you.

Part 3: What Can Be Covered in 5 Hours?

What Can Be Covered in 5 Hours of Wedding Photography?

Five hours gives you considerably more flexibility.

This amount of coverage can work well for couples who want more than just the ceremony and portraits but don't necessarily need photography from the beginning of the day through the end of the reception.

Depending on your timeline, five hours could include:

  • Getting-ready details and final preparations
  • Part of the getting-ready experience
  • Ceremony coverage
  • Family and group photographs
  • Couple portraits
  • Cocktail hour
  • Part of the reception
  • Candid photographs of your guests

The exact combination depends on how your wedding day is structured.

For example, you might choose to begin photography shortly before the ceremony and continue through your portraits and cocktail hour.

Another couple might prefer to begin during the final stages of getting ready and finish after the first part of their reception.

Five hours can be a great middle ground

For couples who don't need full-day coverage, five hours can provide a nice balance between meaningful storytelling and a more focused photography timeline.

The key is planning the start and end times carefully.

If there are specific moments you absolutely want photographed, make those moments the foundation of your coverage.

📍 A Vancouver wedding isn't necessarily a one-location wedding

This is something Vancouver couples should think about when planning their photography timeline.

Your getting-ready location might be somewhere in downtown Vancouver.

Your ceremony could be at a venue in another neighbourhood.

Your portraits might take place at a nearby park or waterfront location.

And your reception could be somewhere else entirely.

Travel time between locations can quickly affect your photography coverage.

That's why your photographer should understand your complete wedding timeline and locations before recommending how many hours you need.

Part 4: What Can Be Covered in 7–8 Hours?

What Can Be Covered in 7–8 Hours of Wedding Photography?

For many couples, 7–8 hours of wedding photography provides enough time to tell a much more complete story of the day.

Rather than focusing only on the ceremony and portraits, this amount of coverage can allow your photographer to document the atmosphere and emotions before, during and after the ceremony.

Depending on your wedding timeline, 7–8 hours could include:

  • Bridal and groom preparations
  • Wedding details and accessories
  • Candid moments with family and wedding party
  • First look, if you're planning one
  • Ceremony
  • Family and group photographs
  • Couple portraits
  • Wedding party portraits
  • Cocktail hour
  • Reception details
  • Guest interactions
  • Speeches and toasts
  • First dance
  • Some of the evening celebration

You don't necessarily need all of these photographed, but having a longer coverage window gives your photographer more opportunities to capture the story naturally.

Why longer coverage can make a difference?

A wedding isn't just a series of major events.

Some of the photographs you treasure most years later may be the moments between them - your best friend fixing your dress, your parents watching from across the room, your partner laughing with a sibling, guests embracing during cocktail hour, or everyone relaxing after the formal photographs are finished.

Those moments require time and attention.

A longer photography timeline gives your photographer the opportunity to look beyond the checklist and document what is actually happening around you.

Part 5: Do You Need Getting-Ready Photography?

Do You Need Getting-Ready Photography

Getting-ready photographs are often overlooked when couples are deciding how much wedding photography coverage they need.

Yet the hours before the ceremony can contain some of the most emotional and personal moments of the entire wedding day.

The final touches to your hair and makeup.

Your wedding dress hanging in the room.

Your rings, shoes, invitations and other meaningful details.

Your wedding party getting ready together.

A parent helping with the final details.

The excitement immediately before leaving for the ceremony.

These photographs help establish the beginning of your wedding story.

Getting-ready photography isn't only about the bride

While bridal preparation is often the focus, photographs of the groom or partner getting ready can be equally meaningful.

Friends adjusting jackets, sharing a drink, fixing a boutonniere, laughing together or simply taking a quiet moment before the ceremony can create a fuller picture of the day.

If both partners are getting ready in different locations, however, you'll need to consider the logistics carefully.

Your photographer may need additional time to travel between locations, or you may want to discuss whether a second photographer would make sense for your timeline.

When should getting-ready coverage begin?

You don't necessarily need your photographer present from the moment you wake up.

For many couples, the most meaningful photographs happen during the final stages of preparation.

This might include:

Hair and makeup finishing touches

Putting on your wedding attire

Jewellery and accessories

Detail photographs

Wedding party interactions

Letters or gifts

Parent or family moments

Final portraits before leaving

The right starting point depends on your schedule and which memories you want preserved.

Part 6: How Much Time Should You Allow for Wedding Portraits?

How Much Time Should You Allow for Wedding Portraits?

Portraits are an important part of your wedding photography, but they don't need to feel like a long formal photoshoot.

The best wedding portraits often happen when couples have enough time to relax, interact and simply be together.

Rather than rushing through a long list of poses, a natural approach allows for a mixture of gently guided portraits and genuine moments.

Couple portraits

For your couple portraits, allowing around 20–45 minutes can provide a comfortable amount of time, depending on your location, lighting and timeline.

You may not need the entire time.

The goal is to create enough breathing room that you're not constantly watching the clock.

A short portrait session can also be divided into smaller moments throughout the day.

For example, you might take a few portraits immediately after the ceremony and then step away again during golden hour for another set of photographs.

Wedding party portraits

Wedding party photographs generally require less time when everyone knows where they need to be.

Having a prepared list of group combinations can make this part of the day much more efficient.

Your photographer can then focus on keeping things organized while still capturing natural interactions between the formal photographs.

Don't underestimate sunset portraits

If your wedding schedule allows it, consider setting aside a few minutes around sunset for additional couple portraits.

Vancouver and the surrounding Lower Mainland offer an incredible variety of natural settings — from waterfront locations and urban environments to forests, gardens and mountain views.

The light can change dramatically throughout the day, which means your wedding portraits don't have to look identical from beginning to end.

Part 7: Don't Forget Family and Group Photos

Don't Forget Family and Group Photos

Family photographs may not feel as exciting as couple portraits when you're planning your wedding, but they can become some of the most valuable photographs you receive.

Your wedding brings people together who may rarely be in the same place at the same time.

Parents.

Grandparents.

Siblings.

Children.

Close friends.

Extended family.

These photographs preserve relationships that are part of your story.

Make a family photo list before the wedding

A simple list can save a surprising amount of time.

Instead of trying to remember every combination on the wedding day, prepare the most important group photographs in advance.

For example:

Couple with both sets of parents

Couple with immediate family

Couple with siblings

Couple with grandparents

Couple with wedding party

Couple with specific close friends

Keep the list focused on the combinations that genuinely matter to you.

Your photographer can then work through the list efficiently while someone from your wedding party helps gather people.

Allow more time than you think

Family photographs can take longer than expected, particularly when guests are moving between locations.

If you have a large family or a long list of combinations, discuss this with your photographer before finalizing your timeline.

A little planning beforehand can make the experience much smoother.

A little Vancouver-specific planning

If your wedding involves multiple locations around Vancouver or the Lower Mainland, don't forget to account for travel time, parking, traffic and venue access.

A photography timeline that looks perfect on paper can quickly become compressed if you're travelling between downtown Vancouver, North Vancouver, Richmond, Burnaby, Surrey, White Rock or another location.

Your photographer should ideally review the complete timeline with you before the wedding so the photography coverage works realistically with the day's logistics.

Part 8: How Your Wedding Timeline Affects Photography Coverage

How Your Wedding Timeline Affects Photography Coverage

Your wedding timeline is one of the best tools for figuring out how much photography coverage you actually need.

Instead of starting with a package and trying to fit your wedding into it, try doing the opposite:

Start with the moments you want photographed, then work backward to determine when your photographer needs to arrive and when they can leave.

For example, imagine your ceremony begins at 3:00 PM.

If you only want photographs of the ceremony, family portraits and a short couple session, you may not need your photographer there all day.

But if you also want photographs of the preparations, details, first look and the excitement before the ceremony, your coverage needs to begin much earlier.

The same applies to the end of the evening.

If speeches and your first dance are important to you, your photographer may need to stay beyond the ceremony and portraits.

If you want photographs of the dance floor, guests celebrating and the atmosphere of your reception, you'll want to include those moments when planning your coverage.

A simple way to plan your coverage

Start by writing down the moments you absolutely don't want to miss.

Then consider:

  • What time does the photography story begin?
  • What time does the ceremony start?
  • How much time is needed for family and wedding party photographs?
  • When will you have your couple portraits?
  • When does the reception begin?
  • Which reception events do you want photographed?
  • What time would you be comfortable ending photography coverage?

Once you have those answers, the number of hours becomes much easier to determine.

Part 9: Intimate Weddings vs. Full-Day Weddings

Intimate Weddings vs. Full-Day Weddings

Not every wedding needs the same photography coverage.

An intimate wedding in Vancouver can have a completely different timeline from a large wedding with hundreds of guests.

Intimate weddings

Smaller weddings often have simpler timelines and fewer locations.

You may have a ceremony, family photographs, couple portraits and an intimate dinner all in one location.

In this situation, shorter coverage can provide everything you need without having to pay for hours that don't add meaningful value.

For example, 3–5 hours may be enough to document the key parts of an intimate celebration, depending on the timeline.

Larger weddings

Larger weddings often involve more people, more formal photographs and more moving parts.

There may also be separate getting-ready locations, transportation between venues, a first look, a large family photo list, cocktail hour, speeches, dinner and dancing.

In these situations, 7–8 hours or more may make more sense because there are simply more moments and logistics to document.

The important thing isn't choosing the package with the most hours.

It's choosing coverage that gives your photographer enough time to tell your story properly.

Part 10: How to Choose the Right Coverage for Your Vancouver Wedding

How to Choose the Right Wedding Photography Coverage

If you're still unsure how many hours you need, start with these four questions.

1. What is the most important part of your wedding day?

For some couples, it's the ceremony.

For others, it's the getting ready, portraits, family, reception or simply having photographs of everyone together.

Identify your priorities first.

2. Do you want the beginning of the story?

If you want photographs of your preparations, details and the people around you before the ceremony, your photography coverage will need to start earlier.

If those moments aren't important to you, you may be able to begin later.

3. Do you want photographs of the reception?

If you want your photographer to capture speeches, first dances, dinner, guests and dancing, you'll need coverage that extends into the evening.

4. How much time do you want for portraits?

Don't forget to leave room for portraits.

A wedding day can become surprisingly busy, and giving yourselves some breathing room can make the experience much more enjoyable.

Your portraits don't have to feel like a long formal photoshoot. A calm, natural approach can create beautiful images while still allowing you to enjoy your wedding day.

Part 11: When More Hours Are Worth It

When More Wedding Photography Hours Are Worth It

More coverage isn't automatically better.

But there are situations where additional hours can make a meaningful difference.

Consider extending your coverage if:

  • You want getting-ready photographs
  • You have multiple locations
  • You are having a first look
  • You have a large family photo list
  • You want plenty of time for couple portraits
  • Your ceremony and reception are separated by significant travel
  • You want cocktail-hour photographs
  • You want speeches photographed
  • You want your first dance captured
  • You want photographs of your guests dancing and celebrating
  • You want a more complete beginning-to-end story of your wedding

Sometimes the extra hours aren't about photographing another major event.

They're about giving the day enough breathing room.

That can make a surprisingly big difference.

Part 12: When You May Not Need Full-Day Coverage

When You May Not Need Full-Day Wedding Photography

There is also nothing wrong with choosing less coverage.

If you're having a small ceremony and intimate celebration, full-day photography may simply not be necessary.

You may not need:

  • Hours of getting-ready coverage
  • Extensive reception coverage
  • Multiple locations
  • A large number of formal group photographs
  • Photographs of the entire evening

If your priority is simply documenting your ceremony, family, portraits and a few meaningful moments afterward, a shorter collection may be a better fit.

Good wedding photography isn't about how many hours you book. It's about whether the coverage captures the memories you care about.

That's the philosophy I bring to Joenell Photography.

Part 13: Building Your Wedding Photography Timeline

How to Build a Wedding Photography Timeline

Once you've decided which parts of your wedding day you want photographed, the next step is creating a timeline that gives those moments enough space.

A good photography timeline isn't about filling every minute with photographs.

It's about creating enough structure that you can enjoy your wedding while still giving your photographer the time needed to document it naturally.

Start with the ceremony

Your ceremony is usually the easiest place to begin.

Write down:

Ceremony start:

Ceremony end:

Then work backward.

If you want photographs of your guests arriving, your wedding party preparing, or the final moments before the ceremony, your photographer will need to arrive earlier.

Allow time for family photographs

Immediately after the ceremony can be a convenient time for family photographs because many of your important people are already together.

Create your family photo list ahead of time and communicate it to your photographer.

This helps keep things moving and prevents your portrait time from disappearing while everyone is being gathered.

Give yourselves breathing room for portraits

Try not to schedule your couple portraits so tightly that you feel like you're racing from one photograph to another.

Even an extra 10 or 15 minutes can make the experience feel completely different.

Your photographer can guide you through the portraits while also leaving room for natural interaction.

Remember travel time

This is particularly important for weddings around Vancouver and the Lower Mainland.

If your getting-ready location, ceremony and reception are in different places, build realistic travel time into your schedule.

Don't assume that a 20-minute drive will always take 20 minutes.

Traffic, parking, loading equipment and finding the correct entrance can all add time.

Plan around the light

Natural light can have a major effect on your portraits.

If you are hoping for soft evening light or sunset photographs, tell your photographer.

Your timeline can sometimes be adjusted to create a short window for additional couple portraits without taking you away from the celebration for too long.

A Sample 8-Hour Wedding Photography Timeline

Every wedding is different, but here's an example of how an 8-hour photography timeline might look:

1:00 PM - Getting ready

Details, final preparations, wedding party and candid moments.

2:30 PM - First look

A private moment together before the ceremony.

2:45 PM - Couple & wedding party portraits

Natural portraits while everyone is together.

3:30 PM - Ceremony

4:00 PM - Family photographs

Immediate family and other important combinations.

4:30 PM - Cocktail hour

Candid photographs, details and guest interactions.

5:30 PM - Reception begins

6:00 PM - Dinner & speeches

7:00 PM - First dance

7:15 PM - Evening celebration

8:30 PM - Photography coverage ends

This is only an example.

Your actual timeline might look completely different depending on your venue, ceremony time, travel, traditions and priorities.

The important thing is to build the photography around your wedding, rather than trying to force your wedding into someone else's timeline.

Part 14: Final Thoughts

So, How Many Hours of Wedding Photography Do You Really Need?

There isn't one perfect answer.

For some couples, 3 hours is enough to beautifully document an intimate ceremony and portraits.

For others, 5 hours provides the right balance between the ceremony, portraits and part of the celebration.

And for couples who want a more complete story, from getting ready through the reception, 7–8 hours or more may be the better choice.

The right amount of coverage depends on:

  • Your wedding size
  • Your timeline
  • Your locations
  • Whether you want getting-ready photographs
  • How much time you want for portraits
  • Your family and group photo requirements
  • Whether you want reception coverage
  • Which moments are most meaningful to you

Rather than asking "What is the cheapest package?", consider asking:

"Which parts of our wedding day would we regret not having photographs of?"

That question can make the decision much easier.

Your wedding photographs aren't simply a record of what happened.

Years from now, they can bring you back to the people who were there, the emotions you felt and the small moments you may have forgotten.

That's why at Joenell Photography, I approach weddings with a focus on natural moments, genuine connection and storytelling.

The goal isn't to photograph every second.

It's to preserve the moments that made your day yours.
