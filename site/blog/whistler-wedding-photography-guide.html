Whistler is one of the most beautiful places in Canada to get married. In one day you can have a lakeside ceremony, portraits in old-growth forest and alpine views at sunset — all about two hours from Vancouver along the Sea to Sky Highway.

I'm Joenell, a Vancouver-based wedding photographer who photographs weddings, elopements and engagement sessions in Whistler and Squamish. This guide covers the best photo spots, how the mountain light works, when to plan your timeline and what to know before you book.

Why Couples Choose Whistler and the Sea to Sky

Whistler and Squamish give you a huge range of scenery within minutes: turquoise lakes, granite cliffs, waterfalls, rainforest and snow-capped peaks. The drive itself — Highway 99 along Howe Sound — is one of the most scenic in the world, and many couples stop at a lookout on the way for extra portraits.

The Best Wedding Photo Spots in Whistler

Lost Lake

A short walk from Whistler Village, Lost Lake offers calm water, forest trails and a dock that's lovely in soft evening light. The municipal park can be booked for events through the Resort Municipality of Whistler.

Green Lake

Famous for its glacial green water and mountain backdrop. Lookouts along the lake make for big, dramatic portraits — especially in the hour before sunset.

Alta Lake and the Stone Circle

The Stone Circle above Alta Lake is a favourite for micro-weddings and elopements, with room for a small group and a beautiful view over the water.

Rainbow Park

A sandy lakeside park with classic views of Whistler and Blackcomb mountains — great for relaxed summer portraits and golden-hour light on the water.

Forest trails near the village

The Valley Trail and the forest around the village give you towering cedars, moss and soft, even light — ideal on cloudy days or in the middle of the afternoon.

Mountaintop venues

Gondola-access venues let you say your vows above the clouds. They come with their own schedules and packages, so book early and plan portraits around the last ride down.

Squamish Spots on the Way

If you'd like more variety, Squamish is about halfway between Vancouver and Whistler. Lookouts over Howe Sound, waterfalls and the Sea to Sky Gondola summit are all within easy reach — perfect for an adventure elopement or a first-look stop before a Whistler ceremony.

Planning Around Mountain Light

In the mountains, the sun drops behind the peaks earlier than the official sunset time, so the valley can lose its direct light sooner than you'd expect. I plan portraits around when the light actually leaves your location, not just the clock.

  • Summer: sunset is around 9:20 pm in late June, so golden-hour portraits often happen after dinner.
  • Winter: sunset is around 4:15 pm in December — plan your ceremony for early afternoon to keep daylight for portraits.
  • Spring and autumn: softer light, fewer crowds and beautiful colour, with a little more weather variety.

A Sample Whistler Wedding Timeline

  • 2:00 pm — getting ready and details
  • 3:30 pm — first look and wedding party photos in the forest
  • 4:30 pm — lakeside ceremony
  • 5:00 pm — family photos and cocktail hour
  • 7:00 pm — dinner and speeches
  • 8:30 pm — golden-hour portraits by the water (summer)

For a smaller wedding, The Story collection (up to 4 hours) usually covers the ceremony through golden hour. For a full day, The Complete Story covers up to 6 hours.

Weather and Backup Plans

Alpine weather changes quickly, even in summer. Every Whistler wedding I photograph has a covered backup and a flexible portrait plan. Cloud and mist often create the most beautiful, moody photos — and snow turns a winter wedding into something unforgettable.

Permits and Bookings

Municipal parks such as Lost Lake, Rainbow Park and the Stone Circle are booked through the Resort Municipality of Whistler, generally from mid-May to mid-October, and events need liability insurance. Provincial parks may require a park use permit. Venues and gondola summits handle their own bookings. I'll help you check what applies to your plans.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far is Whistler from Vancouver?

About two hours by car along the Sea to Sky Highway (Highway 99). Squamish is roughly halfway, about an hour from Vancouver.

What's the best month for a Whistler wedding?

July to September offers the most reliable weather and long evenings. June and early October are quieter with beautiful light, and December to March gives you snow.

Do you photograph winter weddings in Whistler?

Yes. Winter weddings are some of my favourites — snowy forest portraits and cosy indoor light. We plan the timeline around the shorter days.

Can we combine Squamish and Whistler in one day?

Absolutely. Many couples stop at a Squamish lookout for portraits on the way up — just allow time for the drive in your timeline.
